# 8. Why Food-Waste & Sustainability Apps Don't Get Popular

*The question deserves a research answer, not an opinion. There is a body of organisational and
academic work on exactly this, and it is more damning — and more useful — than the trade press.*

---

## 8.1 Yes, there is formal research. Here is what it says.

| Body | What they studied | The finding that matters |
|---|---|---|
| **EU Horizon 2020 — LOWINFOOD** (27 partners, 12 countries) | Trialled the CozZo pantry app in **50 households** across Finland, Austria and Greece | The only public multi-country household trial of a pantry app. Users valued expiry reminders and stock oversight; benefits concentrated in **inexperienced** households. Notably, the published outputs report **no dropout, retention or usage-frequency data** — the numbers a founder most needs |
| **de Almeida Oroski & da Silva (2023)** — mini-review of food-waste-reducing platforms | Systematic review across platform types | *"None of the studies investigated whether platforms succeeded or failed in contributing positively to environmental impacts."* **After a decade, nobody has demonstrated that these products work.** |
| **Frontiers in Psychology (2025)** — systematic review of experimental waste-reduction interventions | Six intervention families, ranked | **Education/information is 53% of all interventions and "often insufficient by itself."** Effects across the field are *"modest and short-term."* But **computerised food-waste tracking achieved a 64% reduction in mass** — in settings with an accountable owner |
| **WRAP** (UK, Love Food Hate Waste) | National campaign, 15+ years, household surveys | The campaign moved avoidable household waste **~5.3%** at national scale, saving ~£700M/yr. And the barrier that should stop a founder cold: **"many people thought they didn't waste significant amounts of food."** |
| **ReFED / JHU / Harvard FLPC (2025)** | US consumer survey | **43%** always/usually discard on the printed date — the single largest addressable behaviour |
| **Systematic review, TPB & household food waste (2025)** | Theory of Planned Behaviour literature | TPB *"primarily focuses on measuring behavioural intentions rather than actual behaviour"* — the field's own instruments measure the wrong thing |
| **Hong et al. (2026)**, *Int. Trans. in Operational Research* | Barriers and motivations in adopting food-waste apps | Dedicated adoption-barrier study; paywalled, but the surrounding literature converges on trust, stigma and habit inertia |
| **BMLEH / Destatis / GfK** (Germany) | National food-waste accounting and household composition study | **10.9m t (2023), 58% from households, ~79 kg/person/yr.** Of *avoidable* waste: **35% fresh produce, 13% bakery, 12% drinks, 9% dairy** — i.e. nearly half the problem has no barcode and no printed date |
| **BMLEH "Zu gut für die Tonne!"** + German National Strategy for Food Waste Reduction | State campaign and SDG 12.3 targets | Germany has already done the *awareness* job — and shipped a **free government app**. ⚠️ **Information-only products have no commercial space in this market**, and the reference price is zero |
| **RCTs**: J. Cleaner Production 2024; Circular Economy & Sustainability 2022; Sustainability 2025 | Household interventions | Information alone: **minimal effect.** Structured intervention **with human coaching**: sustained **~30%** reduction. Implementation intentions beat information |

**Sources are listed in full at the end of this document.**

## 8.2 The seven structural reasons, in order of how much damage each does

### 1. People do not believe they have the problem
WRAP's own research: *many people thought they didn't waste significant amounts of food.*
Meanwhile ReFED measures ~$800/person/year. **The gap between measured waste and perceived
waste is the single largest obstacle in the category**, and it is a *marketing* problem
disguised as a product problem.

You cannot sell a solution to a problem the buyer does not believe they have. Every app in the
graveyard opened with "reduce your food waste" to an audience that had already self-assessed as
low-waste.

> **Implication:** the acquisition promise cannot be waste reduction. It has to be something the
> user already knows they want — dinner solved, money saved, less shopping. Waste reduction is
> the *outcome you report back*, not the pitch.

### 2. The intention–action gap is the field's defining failure
The academic literature is explicit that Theory-of-Planned-Behaviour studies measure *intention*,
and intention does not convert. This is why every survey in this category is encouraging and every
retention chart is not. **Stated intent overstates revealed preference by roughly an order of
magnitude in consumer utility** — which is exactly the error our own TAM funnel made by stacking
two survey-intent percentages (see [§9.9](09-investor-pack.md#99-market-sizing-tamsamsom)).

### 3. Information doesn't work, and information is what everyone builds
53% of all reviewed waste interventions are educational, and they are *"often insufficient by
itself."* Almost every food app in the market is, functionally, an information product: here is
your list, here is a date, here is a tip. The interventions that *did* work were structural —
choice architecture, defaults, commitments, loss framing, and prompts at the decision point.

### 4. The reward is diffuse, delayed and invisible; the cost is immediate
Waste leaves the house €2 at a time and is never billed. Effort is paid up-front, in the kitchen,
with bags in hand. **Every session is a bad trade for the user** unless the app returns something
concrete in the same week.

### 5. Selection inverts the value
Waste concentrates in large, chaotic, time-poor households with children. Adoption concentrates
in conscientious, organised, already-low-waste individuals. **The people who install need it
least; the people who need it most never install.** The LOWINFOOD finding that benefits
concentrated in *inexperienced* households is the same fact from the other direction — and no
consumer app in this market is designed for a chaotic family kitchen.

### 6. Nobody has proved it works, so nobody can market it
*"None of the studies investigated whether platforms succeeded or failed."* The field has no
credible efficacy claim. Contrast weight-loss, fitness or savings apps, which can all point to a
number. The reviewers of the Frontiers study call this the **"streetlamp effect"** — electricity
and water dominate the research because utilities hand over the data; food waste is understudied
because measuring it is expensive. **The absence of evidence is itself a commercial barrier.**

### 7. Technology without business-model alignment
The mini-review is blunt: digital platforms *"cannot succeed without robust business model
alignment addressing value creation, delivery, and capture."* Prevention apps create value
(less waste) at a point where **no transaction occurs**, so they cannot capture it. Marketplaces
(Too Good To Go) create value *at a transaction* and capture it trivially.

## 8.3 The comparison that explains everything

The same technology — computerised food-waste tracking — produces a **64% reduction in mass** in
commercial kitchens and near-nothing in homes. It is not the software that differs.

| | Commercial kitchen (works) | Home kitchen (doesn't) |
|---|---|---|
| Is waste measured? | Yes, by weight, daily | No, never |
| Does anyone own the number? | Yes — a chef with a P&L | Nobody |
| Is there a budget line? | Yes | No |
| Who pays for the software? | The person accountable for the loss | The person experiencing the loss, out of pocket |
| Time to feel the benefit | Next month's food cost | Vague, eventually |
| Is using it part of the job? | Yes, it is work | No, it is *extra* work |

> **Winnow deployed across 800 Marriott kitchens. No consumer pantry app has ever reached
> comparable penetration in a comparable segment.** The category's success is real — it just
> lives in B2B, where waste is a measured cost with an accountable owner.

**This is the most important strategic fact in this document.** Any consumer play in this space is
attempting to manufacture accountability where none exists. That is possible, but it must be a
deliberate design goal, not an assumption.

## 8.4 What is actually missing in the market

Nine gaps, each stated as a testable opportunity rather than a slogan. Ranked by size of gap ×
our ability to fill it.

### Gap 1 — Nobody has solved the "out" side ⭐ *the biggest*
Every product on the market solves *getting food in* (barcode, receipt, loyalty sync, camera).
**None models consumption.** So every inventory drifts, every alert eventually lies, and the
category's core failure mode is structurally guaranteed. **Repurchase cadence is sitting in the
receipt data unused** — see [§5.4b](05-technical-architecture.md). This is the clearest piece of
genuinely unclaimed ground in the market.

### Gap 2 — Nobody serves the high-waste household ⭐
Products are designed for tidy people: clean lists, complete inventories, satisfying categories.
The high-waste household is chaotic, has children, shops irregularly, cooks under time pressure,
and will never curate anything. **A product designed for mess, not for order, does not exist.**

### Gap 3 — Prevention has no transaction ⭐
Value is created where no money changes hands. Everyone therefore falls back on a ~€3/month
subscription for a product used two minutes a week — the hardest sale in consumer software.
**The unclaimed position is prevention that attaches to the next purchase**: the app that knows
what you have is uniquely qualified to build what you need, and that basket is a real, aligned
revenue event (see [§9.6](09-investor-pack.md#96-unit-economics)).

### Gap 4 — Nobody has proved efficacy, so the first credible proof is a moat
No platform has published a defensible reduction figure. A product that runs a proper
control-arm study on its own users and publishes the result would own the only evidence-based
claim in the category — and it is the entry ticket to the channels that matter (councils, WRAP,
retailers, insurers, ESG programmes).

### Gap 5 — Date-label confusion is unowned
43% of households discard on the printed date. "Use by" vs "best before" is legally distinct and
publicly misunderstood. **Nobody is the trusted consumer authority on this.** It is also the
highest-SEO-value, highest-utility content in the domain, and it maps exactly onto the shelf-life
data asset the product needs anyway.

### Gap 6 — Leftovers and home-cooked food are entirely unserved
The shortest-fuse, highest-guilt, highest-frequency waste category. It appears on no receipt,
has no barcode, and is deferred to "later" by every product including, originally, ours.

### Gap 7 — Retailer incentives are misaligned and nobody has bridged them
Retailers profit from over-purchase; loyalty-integrated apps (Cooklist) inherit that conflict.
A **cross-retailer, non-retailer-owned** product that credibly refuses to sell shopping data can
occupy a trust position no retailer-integrated competitor can.

### Gap 8 — The freezer is the most-used real rescue and the worst-supported feature
Freezing is what people actually do when food is about to go. Unlabelled freezing is then a
leading cause of freezer waste. Every product treats the freezer as a storage-location dropdown.

### Gap 9 — Nothing intervenes at the moment of purchase
Prevention's highest-leverage moment is *not buying the third bag of spinach*. It happens in the
aisle, offline, one-handed, in ninety seconds. Almost every product is designed for the kitchen.

## 8.5 What this means for our plan

| Research finding | Direct consequence |
|---|---|
| People don't believe they waste | **Stop leading with waste.** Lead with dinner, money and "don't buy it twice". Report waste reduction *after* the fact |
| Intention ≠ action | **No survey-based validation.** Only revealed behaviour: the concierge test, and pre-sales |
| Information doesn't work | The product is a **prompt at a decision point**, not a dashboard |
| Coaching works (~30%) | The concierge test isn't just validation — **it is the intervention with the best evidence base**, and it may be the product |
| Selection inverts value | Recruit and design for **high-waste, chaotic households** |
| Nobody has proved efficacy | **Run a control arm from beta onward.** Cheap, and it becomes the moat |
| Value created where no transaction occurs | The **grocery hand-off is not a "second leg" for month 12** — it may be the primary model |
| B2B works because someone owns the number | Keep a **B2B/institutional option open** (Bezirke, insurers, retailers, appliance makers) rather than pretending it's beneath us |
| 🇩🇪 **In Germany the price is anchored at zero** by foodsharing.de and a free state app | **Justify the paid tier by convenience, never by virtue.** A "do good" pitch competes with a volunteer movement and loses |
| 🇩🇪 **SirPlus — a famous Berlin food-waste brand with real stores — went through insolvency** | The local proof of §8.3. **Address it before an investor raises it**: SirPlus was redistribution with inventory and logistics risk; prevention has neither. That failure mode is not one we can have |
